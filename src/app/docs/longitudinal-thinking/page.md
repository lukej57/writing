{% callout title="TL;DR" type="note" %}
AI can outmatch a person in an immediate timeframe.
Over time a person is smarter, because the thinking is longitudinal.
A problem takes hold, and reality keeps testing it.
{% /callout %}

Related: [Invariant Wisdom](/docs/invariant-wisdom).

In the moment, AI can outmatch a human intellect.
That is the wrong comparison.
A person does not finish a thought in one sitting.

You engage with a problem.
It takes hold in the subconscious.
You keep moving through reality.
The idea is tested, and it evolves, again and again.
That is a long evolutionary process.
It can include revolutions.

Kuhn described the collective form in *The Structure of Scientific Revolutions*.
Scientists gather knowledge.
What we know faces pressure from reality.
Evolution runs until it hits a dead end.
Then a revolution is required.

The personal form is classical dialectic.
A person inhabits an idea and puts it to the test of their own experience, over time.
Reality puts pressure on that worldview, as it does on a science.
You see where it leads.
You embody the idea, and you watch how it plays out.

Matthew 7:16: you shall know them by their fruits.
You can know the idea by its fruit.

A theory that survives that is strong enough to found lasting action.
A strong theoretical foundation can motivate that action, and sustain it, toward a vision.
This, something AI cannot do.

That is the thinking a human being can do.
It is far superior to the pattern matching AI can do.

An open question, left open.
Give a language model a robotic body, and the science-fiction cases like it.
How far could it go?

It may be true that AI shifts a software developer toward product design and the concrete details of the problem.
It should also shift you toward longitudinal thinking.

Longitudinal thinking is a sequence of small arcs over time.
AI can accelerate the small arcs.
You can run a mini-dialectic with it, quickly.
The small pieces of the journey go faster.

Introducing language models changes the calculus of what is valuable.
Through a utilitarian lens, a lot of skill and knowledge is worth less.
The question is where you shift.

Iain McGilchrist's two modes of attention are one answer.
The right hemisphere takes in the whole, intuitively.
A language model cannot do that, and will not be able to.
It is a distinctly human ability.
It may be more valuable than ever.

AI does not make us less busy.
Anything but.
It may free us from analytical work.
Most of the time, attention can shift toward the intuitive mode.
